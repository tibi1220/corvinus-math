% title: Meeting for lunch
% tags: independent-rv, uniform, joint-continuous
% ---

\begin{problem}
	Two colleagues agree to meet for lunch between 12:00 and 13:00. Each of
	them arrives at a time that is uniformly distributed over the hour,
	independently of the other. Whoever arrives first waits 10 minutes for the
	other, then leaves. Let $X$ and $Y$ be their arrival times in minutes after
	12:00.
	(a) Find the joint density of $X$ and $Y$.
	(b) Find the probability that they meet, $P(|X-Y|\le 10)$.
\end{problem}

\begin{solution}
	\idea Independent random variables have a joint density that is the product
	of the marginal densities. Here the product is constant on a square, so a
	probability is simply (area of the region)$\,\times\,$(the constant). We
	find the area by geometry instead of a double integral.

	\step{(a)} $X$ is uniform on $[0,60]$, so its density is
	\[
		f_X(x)=\begin{cases}
			\dfrac{1}{60} & 0\le x\le 60, \\[6pt]
			0             & \text{otherwise,}
		\end{cases}
	\]
	and $Y$ has the same density $f_Y(y)=\frac{1}{60}$ on $[0,60]$. The two
	arrival times are independent, so the joint density is the product of the
	marginals:
	\[
		f(x,y)=f_X(x)\,f_Y(y)=\frac{1}{60}\cdot\frac{1}{60}=\frac{1}{3600}
		\qquad\text{for } 0\le x\le 60,\ 0\le y\le 60,
	\]
	and $f(x,y)=0$ outside this square. Check: the square has area
	$60\cdot60=3600$, so the total probability is $3600\cdot\frac1{3600}=1$.

	\step{(b)} The first to arrive waits 10 minutes. So they meet exactly when
	the two arrival times are at most 10 minutes apart: $|X-Y|\le10$, that is,
	\[
		x-10\le y\le x+10 .
	\]
	In the $(x,y)$-plane this is the band between the lines $y=x-10$ and
	$y=x+10$ (shaded below).
	\begin{center}
		\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.075,>=stealth]
			\fill[teal!30] (0,0)--(10,0)--(60,50)--(60,60)--(50,60)--(0,10)--cycle;
			\draw[thick] (0,0) rectangle (60,60);
			\draw[navy,thick] (10,0)--(60,50);
			\draw[navy,thick] (0,10)--(50,60);
			\draw[gray,dashed] (0,0)--(60,60);
			\draw[->] (0,0)--(68,0) node[right] {$x$};
			\draw[->] (0,0)--(0,68) node[above] {$y$};
			\node[below] at (0,0) {$0$};
			\node[below] at (10,0) {$10$};
			\node[below] at (60,0) {$60$};
			\node[left] at (0,10) {$10$};
			\node[left] at (0,60) {$60$};
			\node at (42,15) {\small no meeting};
			\node at (18,45) {\small no meeting};
			\node[navy,right] at (60,50) {\small $y=x-10$};
			\node[navy,above] at (50,60) {\small $y=x+10$};
		\end{tikzpicture}
	\end{center}
	Because the joint density is the constant $\frac{1}{3600}$ on the square,
	\[
		P(|X-Y|\le10)=\frac{\text{area of the band}}{3600}.
	\]
	The band is easiest to get through its complement. Outside the band there
	are two right triangles, one in each corner. The lower one has its corners
	at $(10,0)$, $(60,0)$ and $(60,50)$: both legs have length $50$, so its area
	is $\frac12\cdot50\cdot50=1250$. By symmetry (swap the roles of $x$ and
	$y$) the upper triangle has the same area. Together the two triangles have
	area $2\cdot1250=2500$. So
	\[
		\text{area of the band}=3600-2500=1100,
	\]
	and
	\[
		P(|X-Y|\le10)=\frac{1100}{3600}=\frac{11}{36}\approx0.306 .
	\]
	The same result in one line: the two triangles together make one
	$50$-by-$50$ square, so
	\[
		P(|X-Y|\le10)=1-\frac{50^2}{60^2}=1-\left(\frac{50}{60}\right)^2
		=1-\frac{25}{36}=\frac{11}{36}.
	\]

	\begin{answer}
		(a) $f(x,y)=\frac{1}{3600}$ for $0\le x\le60$, $0\le y\le60$, and $0$
		otherwise \quad
		(b) $P(|X-Y|\le10)=\frac{11}{36}\approx0.306$
	\end{answer}
	\begin{remark}
		The double integral gives the same answer, but it is longer: the band
		has to be split into pieces, because the bounds for $y$ change at
		$x=10$ and at $x=50$. With a constant joint density, a probability is
		an area times the constant, and simple geometry is the fastest route.
		\textbf{Common mistake:} computing only $P(X-Y\le10)$ or forgetting one
		of the two triangles. Either colleague can arrive first, so both
		corners must be removed.
	\end{remark}
\end{solution}

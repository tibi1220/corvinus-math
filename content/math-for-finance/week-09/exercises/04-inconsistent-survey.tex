% ---
% title: A survey that cannot be right
% tags: inclusion-exclusion
% ---

\begin{problem}
	A magazine surveyed its 1000 subscribers and reported the following counts:
	312 are professionals, 470 are married, 525 are college graduates,
	42 are professional college graduates, 147 are married college graduates,
	86 are married professionals, and 25 are married professional college
	graduates. Show that these numbers cannot all be correct.
\end{problem}

\begin{solution}
	\idea Count the subscribers who have at least one of the three properties
	with the inclusion-exclusion formula for three sets. If the result is
	larger than the number of all subscribers, the data contradict each other.
	\step{Events} Pick one of the 1000 subscribers at random, each with the same
	probability. Let $A$ be the event that the subscriber is a professional,
	$B$ that the subscriber is married, and $C$ that the subscriber is a college
	graduate. Each probability is the count divided by 1000:
	\[
		P(A)=0.312,\quad P(B)=0.470,\quad P(C)=0.525,
	\]
	\[
		P(AC)=0.042,\quad P(BC)=0.147,\quad P(AB)=0.086,\quad P(ABC)=0.025.
	\]
	\step{Inclusion-exclusion} The event $A\cup B\cup C$ means ``the subscriber
	has at least one of the three properties''. In the sum
	$P(A)+P(B)+P(C)$ a subscriber who belongs to two of the sets is counted
	twice, so we subtract the pairwise intersections. Then a subscriber in all
	three sets was added 3 times and subtracted 3 times, so we add $P(ABC)$
	back once:
	\[
		\begin{aligned}
			P(A\cup B\cup C)
			&=P(A)+P(B)+P(C)-P(AB)-P(AC)-P(BC)+P(ABC)\\
			&=0.312+0.470+0.525-0.086-0.042-0.147+0.025\\
			&=1.307-0.275+0.025=1.057.
		\end{aligned}
	\]
	\step{Contradiction} Every probability is at most $1$, because an event is
	a subset of $\Om$ and $P(\Om)=1$. But we got $P(A\cup B\cup C)=1.057>1$.
	In counts: at least one property would hold for
	\[
		312+470+525-86-42-147+25=1057
	\]
	subscribers, but the magazine has only 1000 subscribers. So at least one
	of the reported numbers is wrong.
	\begin{answer}
		The reported numbers give $1057>1000$ subscribers with at least one of
		the properties, that is, $P(A\cup B\cup C)=1.057>1$, which is
		impossible.
	\end{answer}
	\begin{remark}
		Each number on its own looks fine. We can even fill in a Venn diagram:
		17 are professional graduates but not married ($42-25$), 122 are married
		graduates but not professionals ($147-25$), 61 are married professionals
		without a degree ($86-25$), and then 209 are only professionals, 262
		only married and 361 only graduates. No region is negative. The error
		shows up only when we add all seven regions: $1057$, more than the
		1000 people in the survey.
	\end{remark}
\end{solution}

% title: Insurance claims in a day
% tags: finance, poisson, expectation, variance

\begin{problem}
	\begin{context}
		An insurance company has many policyholders. Each policy leads to a
		\emph{claim} (a request for payment after a loss) only rarely, and the
		policyholders act independently of each other. The number of claims
		that arrive in a day is then usually modeled with a Poisson
		distribution. The insurer uses this model to plan staff and cash
		reserves.
	\end{context}
	The number $X$ of claims an insurance office receives on a day has a
	Poisson distribution with parameter $\lambda=3$.
	(a) What is the probability that no claim arrives on a day?
	(b) What is the probability that exactly 2 claims arrive?
	(c) What is the probability that at least 2 claims arrive?
	(d) Find $\E[X]$ and $\SD(X)$.
\end{problem}

\begin{solution}
	\idea The count of rare events (claims) in a fixed period (a day) is
	modeled by the Poisson distribution, and here it is given directly with
	$\lambda=3$. Each part is one use of the Poisson pmf
	$P(X=i)=e^{-\lambda}\lambda^i/i!$ or of the facts
	$\E[X]=\Var(X)=\lambda$.

	\step{(a)} No claim means $X=0$. Put $i=0$ into the pmf. Since
	$3^0=1$ and $0!=1$,
	\[ P(X=0)=e^{-3}\,\frac{3^0}{0!}=e^{-3}\approx0.0498. \]
	So on about 5\% of the days no claim arrives.

	\step{(b)} Exactly 2 claims means $X=2$. With $3^2=9$ and $2!=2$,
	\[ P(X=2)=e^{-3}\,\frac{3^2}{2!}=\frac92\,e^{-3}\approx0.2240. \]

	\step{(c)} ``At least 2'' means $X=2,3,4,\dots$: infinitely many values.
	Its complement, $X\le1$, has only two values, $X=0$ and $X=1$, so it is
	much shorter to compute the complement and subtract it from 1. The events
	$\{X=0\}$ and $\{X=1\}$ are mutually exclusive, so their probabilities
	add. With $P(X=1)=e^{-3}\cdot3^1/1!=3e^{-3}$,
	\[ P(X\ge2)=1-P(X=0)-P(X=1)=1-e^{-3}-3e^{-3}=1-4e^{-3}\approx1-0.1991
		=0.8009. \]

	\step{(d)} For a Poisson random variable the expected value and the
	variance are both equal to the parameter:
	\[ \E[X]=\lambda=3,\qquad \Var(X)=\lambda=3. \]
	The standard deviation is the square root of the variance:
	$\SD(X)=\sqrt3\approx1.732$. So the office gets 3 claims a day on
	average, and the daily number typically differs from 3 by about 1.7.
	\begin{answer}
		(a) $e^{-3}\approx0.0498$ \quad
		(b) $\frac92e^{-3}\approx0.2240$ \quad
		(c) $1-4e^{-3}\approx0.8009$ \quad
		(d) $\E[X]=3$, $\Var(X)=3$, $\SD(X)=\sqrt3\approx1.732$
	\end{answer}
	\begin{remark}
		\textbf{Common mistake:} in (c), computing $1-P(X=0)-P(X=1)$ but
		forgetting $P(X=1)$, or computing $1-P(X\le2)$. ``At least 2'' still
		contains the value 2, so only 0 and 1 are left out. Another frequent slip
		is $\SD(X)=3$: for the Poisson distribution it is the \emph{variance}
		that equals $\lambda$, and the standard deviation is $\sqrt\lambda$.
	\end{remark}
\end{solution}

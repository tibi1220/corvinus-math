% title: Five cards

\begin{problem}
	Five cards are dealt from a well-shuffled deck of 52 cards. Find the
	probability that the hand contains
	(a) exactly 2 spades and 3 diamonds;
	(b) at least one ace.
\end{problem}

\begin{slides}
	\small
	\exstatement
	\pause
	\sol Outcome: a hand of 5 cards (order does not matter):
	$|\Om|=\binom{52}{5}=2\,598\,960$.
	\pause\\[1mm]
	Well shuffled $\Rightarrow$ every hand is equally likely, so
	$P(E)=\dfrac{|E|}{|\Om|}$.
	\pause\\[1mm]
	(a) Choose the 2 spades from the 13 spades: $\binom{13}{2}=78$ ways.
	\pause\\
	Choose the 3 diamonds from the 13 diamonds: $\binom{13}{3}=286$ ways.
	\pause\\
	Every choice of spades combines with every choice of diamonds:
	\[ P(\text{2 spades, 3 diamonds})=\frac{78\cdot286}{2\,598\,960}
		=\frac{22\,308}{2\,598\,960}\approx0.00858. \]
\end{slides}

\begin{slides}
	\small
	(b) ``At least one ace'' $=$ 1, 2, 3 or 4 aces: four cases to count.
	\pause\\
	The complement is a single case: \alert{no ace}.
	\pause\\
	No ace: all 5 cards come from the 48 non-aces:
	\[ |\{\text{no ace}\}|=\binom{48}{5}=1\,712\,304. \]
	\pause
	\[ P(\text{at least one ace})=1-P(\text{no ace})
		=1-\frac{1\,712\,304}{2\,598\,960}\approx1-0.6588=0.3412. \]
	\pause
	\alert{Wrong:} ``pick an ace, then any 4 other cards'':
	$4\binom{51}{4}\big/\binom{52}{5}\approx0.385$.\\
	A hand with two aces is counted twice (once for each ace as ``the'' ace).
\end{slides}

\begin{solution}
	\idea All $\binom{52}{5}$ hands are equally likely, so every probability is
	a count divided by $\binom{52}{5}$. In (b) count the complement (``no ace''),
	which is a single case.

	\step{The sample space.} An outcome is the set of 5 cards in the hand; the
	order in which the cards arrive does not matter. The number of hands is
	\[ |\Om|=\binom{52}{5}=2\,598\,960. \]
	The deck is well shuffled, so no hand is more likely than any other: all
	outcomes are equally likely. Then, for any event $E$,
	\[ P(E)=\frac{|E|}{|\Om|}=\frac{\text{number of hands in }E}{2\,598\,960}. \]

	\step{(a)} A hand with exactly 2 spades and 3 diamonds is built in two
	stages. First choose which 2 of the 13 spades are in the hand:
	$\binom{13}{2}=78$ ways. Then choose which 3 of the 13 diamonds are in the
	hand: $\binom{13}{3}=286$ ways. Any choice of spades can be combined with
	any choice of diamonds, so by the basic counting principle there are
	$78\cdot286=22\,308$ such hands. The hand has $2+3=5$ cards, so no other
	card fits in. Hence
	\[ P(\text{2 spades and 3 diamonds})
		=\frac{\binom{13}{2}\binom{13}{3}}{\binom{52}{5}}
		=\frac{78\cdot286}{2\,598\,960}=\frac{22\,308}{2\,598\,960}
		\approx0.00858. \]

	\step{(b)} ``At least one ace'' means 1, 2, 3 or 4 aces. Counting it
	directly needs four cases. Its complement, ``no ace'', is a single case, so
	we use $P(E)=1-P(E^c)$.

	A hand with no ace has all 5 cards from the $52-4=48$ cards that are not
	aces:
	\[ |\{\text{no ace}\}|=\binom{48}{5}=1\,712\,304,\qquad
		P(\text{no ace})=\frac{1\,712\,304}{2\,598\,960}\approx0.6588. \]
	Therefore
	\[ P(\text{at least one ace})=1-\frac{1\,712\,304}{2\,598\,960}
		=\frac{886\,656}{2\,598\,960}\approx0.3412. \]
	\begin{answer}
		(a) $\dfrac{22\,308}{2\,598\,960}\approx0.00858$ \quad
		(b) $\dfrac{886\,656}{2\,598\,960}\approx0.3412$
	\end{answer}
	\begin{remark}
		\textbf{Common mistake:} in (b), ``choose one ace ($4$ ways), then any 4
		of the other 51 cards'', giving $4\binom{51}{4}\big/\binom{52}{5}\approx
		0.385$. This counts a hand with two aces twice: once with the first ace
		as ``the chosen ace'' and once with the second. Hands with three or four
		aces are counted three or four times. The result is too large. The
		complement avoids the problem.
	\end{remark}
	\begin{remark}
		The direct count also works if we split by the number of aces:
		\[ \binom41\binom{48}{4}+\binom42\binom{48}{3}+\binom43\binom{48}{2}
			+\binom44\binom{48}{1}=778\,320+103\,776+4\,512+48=886\,656, \]
		the same number as above, with more work.
	\end{remark}
\end{solution}
